Q1 Routine

Antenatal Care — Physiological Pregnancy

  • First visit: full history, BMI, BP, blood type, Rh
  • Labs: CBC, ferritin, OGTT, HBsAg, HIV, RPR, TPHA
  • Serology: anti-HIV, rubella IgG, toxo IgG
  • USG: 11–13+6 wks (NT), 20–21+6 wks (anatomy)
  • Supplements: Folic acid 0.4mg/d until 12wks, Iodine 150mcg/d
  • Anemia threshold: Hb <11 g/dL; Ferritin <30 → treat
  • Rh-negative: Anti-D immunoglobulin at 28 wks
  • Hypertension threshold: ≥140/90 (severe ≥160/110)
Q2 Important

Gestational Diabetes

  • Screen at: 24–28 wks with 75g OGTT
  • Diagnosis (≥1 value): Fasting ≥5.1, 1h ≥10.0, 2h ≥8.5 mmol/L
  • RF: BMI ≥25, age ≥35, prior GDM, PCOS, family hx CD
  • 1st line: Diet + exercise + self-monitoring
  • Targets: Fasting <5.3, 1h postmeal <7.8, 2h <6.7
  • 2nd line: Metformin or insulin if targets not met
  • Fetal risk: Macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, neonatal hypoglycemia
  • Postpartum: OGTT at 6–12 weeks; repeat every 3 years
Q3 Important

Fetal Anomalies, Growth & Wellbeing

  • I trimester screen: NT + PAPP-A + free β-hCG (11–13+6 wks)
  • Down syndrome: ↓PAPP-A + ↑β-hCG + ↑NT
  • Trisomy 18/13: ↓PAPP-A + ↓β-hCG
  • NIPT: cell-free fetal DNA, for high-risk only
  • Amniocentesis: >16 wks; CVS: 11–13+6 wks
  • Growth: Fundal height every visit from 16–18 wks
  • Fetal movements: Normal ≥10 in 2 hours
  • CTG reactive: ≥2 accelerations of ≥15 bpm in 20 min
  • BPP: CTG + USG, max 10 points
Q4 Important

Physiological Childbirth — Stages of Labor

  • Stage 1 — Latent: 0–3 cm, irregular contractions
  • Stage 1 — Active: 3–7 cm, strong regular contractions
  • Stage 1 — Transition: 7–10 cm, full dilation
  • Stage 2: Full dilation → baby delivery (pushing)
  • Stage 3: Placenta delivery (5–30 min physiologically)
  • Stage 4: First 2h postpartum — PPH risk highest
  • Active admission: ≥6 cm dilation
  • Partograph: tracks dilation, descent, contractions, FHR
  • 3rd stage mgmt: Oxytocin 10 IU IM after delivery
Q5 Routine

Postpartum Period & Contraception

  • Duration: 6 weeks after placenta delivery
  • Lochia: Rubra (d1–4) → Serosa (d4–10) → Alba (wks)
  • Uterine involution: 1 cm/day, not palpable at 2 wks
  • Uterine atony → PPH: most common early postpartum issue
  • Safe while breastfeeding: Condoms, POP, IUD, implant
  • AVOID while breastfeeding: Combined pill (↓milk, ↑VTE)
  • Lactational amenorrhea: <6 months, exclusive BF, amenorrhea
  • Copper IUD: can insert immediately postpartum
Q6 Routine

Lactation Physiology & Breastfeeding

  • Trigger: Delivery of placenta → ↓progesterone → milk production
  • Prolactin reflex: Suckling → ↓dopamine → prolactin → milk synthesis
  • Oxytocin reflex: Suckling → oxytocin → myoepithelial contraction → milk ejection
  • Colostrum: days 1–7, rich in IgA, growth factors, Vit A&K
  • WHO: Exclusive BF to 6 months, continue to 2 years
  • BF benefits (baby): ↓infections, ↓allergy, ↓SIDS, cognitive dev
  • BF benefits (mother): ↓PPH, ↓breast/ovarian Ca, ↓Type 2 DM
  • Engorgement: frequent feeds, warm before, cold after, pump PRN
Q7 Important

TORCH Infections in Pregnancy

  • T — Toxoplasmosis (cats, raw meat)
  • O — Others: Syphilis, VZV, HIV, Parvovirus B19
  • R — Rubella (NOT measles!)
  • C — Cytomegalovirus (CMV)
  • H — Herpes simplex (HSV)
  • Primary infection = highest risk (no maternal IgG)
  • IgG+IgM- = old infection, protected. IgG+IgM+ = recent, investigate
  • Rubella syndrome: deafness, cataracts, heart defects (Gregg triad)
  • VZV primary in preg: give VZIG within 96h; treat with Acyclovir
  • HSV at delivery: active lesions → Caesarean
  • HIV: ART throughout; VL <50 copies → vaginal delivery possible
Q8 Critical

Bleeding — Second Half of Pregnancy

  • Placenta Previa: Painless bleeding, soft uterus, wrong lie
  • Abruptio Placentae: Painful bleeding, rigid uterus, hypertonic contractions
  • Vasa Previa: bleeding + fetal bradycardia at ROM
  • Uterine rupture: scar pain, fetal distress, station loss
  • KEY RULE: Suspect PP → vaginal exam CONTRAINDICATED
  • First investigation: Transabdominal USG to locate placenta
  • Rh-negative: always give anti-D immunoglobulin
  • Transport: left lateral decubitus position

Q9 Critical

Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

  • Definition: >500 mL vaginal / >1000 mL caesarean / >2000 mL massive
  • 4T mnemonic: Tone 70% / Trauma 20% / Tissue 10% / Thrombin 1%
  • Most common: Uterine atony
Treatment Sequence
1Oxytocin 10 IU IM/IV (first line)
2Ergometrine / Methylergonovine (not in hypertension)
3Tranexamic acid (within 3h)
4B-Lynch sutures / uterine artery embolization
5Hysterectomy (last resort)
Q10 Important

Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy

  • Chronic HT: known before 20 wks
  • Gestational HT: new after 20 wks, no proteinuria
  • Preeclampsia: HT + proteinuria or organ dysfunction
  • Eclampsia: Preeclampsia + seizures
  • Treat when: ≥140/90; urgently at ≥160/110
Antihypertensives in Pregnancy
Labetalol Nifedipine (long-acting) Methyldopa ACE inhibitors ✗ ARBs ✗ Spironolactone ✗
Q11 Critical

Preeclampsia

  • Diagnosis: BP ≥140/90 after 20 wks + proteinuria ≥300mg/24h or organ dysfunction
  • Severe features: BP ≥160/110, CNS symptoms, HELLP, pulmonary edema
  • Prevention (high risk): Aspirin 150mg/night from 12 wks
  • MgSO₄: 4g IV bolus → 1–2g/h; seizure prophylaxis + eclampsia Rx
  • BP target: SAS <150, DAS 80–100 mmHg
  • Deliver at ≥37 wks; if severe → after stabilization regardless of GA
  • Corticosteroids: if <34–35 wks → betamethasone 12mg IM x2
Q12 Pharma

Epilepsy & Medication Safety

  • Valproate: CONTRAINDICATED — neural tube defects, NDD, autism
  • Safest AEDs: Lamotrigine, Levetiracetam (monotherapy preferred)
  • Folic acid: 4–5 mg/day in epilepsy (standard 0.4mg)
  • Seizure in labor: IV Lorazepam first line
  • Eclampsia: MgSO₄ (not AED)
  • Breastfeeding: most AEDs safe, monitor infant for sedation
  • Valproate in BF: technically low milk levels but avoid if possible
Q13 Important

DVT / Pulmonary Embolism

  • Virchow's triad in pregnancy: hypercoagulation + venous stasis + vessel injury
  • Diagnosis DVT: compression US + D-dimer (often elevated in preg)
  • Diagnosis PE: CT pulmonary angiography (safe in preg, low fetal radiation)
  • Treatment: LMWH (enoxaparin 1mg/kg BD) throughout pregnancy + 6wks postpartum
  • NOT in preg: Warfarin (teratogenic), NOACs (dabigatran, rivaroxaban)
  • Prophylaxis: mobility, hydration; LMWH if high risk
  • High risk: prior VTE, thrombophilia, immobility, CS, OHSS
Q14 Critical

Preterm Labor

  • Definition: 22+0 to 36+6 weeks gestation
  • Diagnosis: contractions 4/20min or 8/60min + cervical changes
  • Cervical length: >25mm → unlikely; <15mm → high risk
  • fFN: if negative → very low risk of delivery in 7 days
  • Tocolytics (48h window): Nifedipine or Atosiban
  • Steroids: Betamethasone 12mg IM x2 at 24–33+6 wks
  • MgSO₄: neuroprotection if 24–32 wks
  • Antibiotics: if PPROM or GBS carrier
  • Cerclage: if cervical insufficiency or short cervix + prior PTL
Q15 Important

Caesarean Section — Indications & Risks

  • Absolute indications: Placenta previa totalis, cephalopelvic disproportion
  • Planned CS: Placenta accreta, prior classical incision, transverse lie, genital HSV at labor
  • Emergency CS: Fetal distress, cord prolapse, abruption with bleeding, uterine rupture
  • Short-term risks: Hemorrhage, infection, anesthesia complications, bladder injury, VTE
  • Long-term risks: Adhesions, placenta accreta in future, uterine rupture risk
  • Neonatal risks: Transient tachypnea, altered gut microbiome, ↑allergy risk
Q16 Important

Postpartum Endometritis

  • Definition: Infection of decidua (pregnancy endometrium)
  • Most common RF: Caesarean section, prolonged labor, PPROM, chorioamnionitis
  • Diagnosis: Fever ≥38°C after 24h + uterine tenderness + malodorous lochia
  • Organisms: Polymicrobial — E.coli, Streptococcus, Bacteroides
  • Treatment: Clindamycin + Gentamicin (IV); Ampicillin-Sulbactam alternative
  • Expect improvement in 48–72h; if not → add Ampicillin or Vancomycin
  • Prophylaxis: Antibiotics before CS skin incision
Q17 Critical

  • Normal neonatal HR: 120–160 bpm

  • Apgar ≠ indication to start resuscitation — clinical assessment is

G1Routine

Gynecological Examination Methods

  • Speculum exam: cervix, vaginal walls, discharge character
  • Bimanual: uterus size/shape/mobility, adnexa, DzK deviation
  • TVUS: 1st choice for pelvic organs, better resolution than TAUS
  • TAUS: large masses, overview, full bladder needed
  • MRI: adenomyosis, deep endometriosis, cervical Ca staging
  • Hysteroscopy: direct uterine cavity visualization + biopsy
  • Laparoscopy: gold standard for endometriosis diagnosis
  • HSG: tubal patency assessment in infertility
G2Routine

Contraception — Efficacy & Contraindications

  • Most effective: Implant, IUS, IUD (<1% failure)
  • COC: ↓menstrual pain, ↓ovarian Ca; CI: thrombosis, migraine+aura, active liver disease
  • POP (mini-pill): safe in BF, migraine, ↑VTE risk patients
  • Emergency contraception: Levonorgestrel ≤72h; Ulipristal ≤120h
  • Copper IUD: most effective EC if inserted ≤5 days
  • Breastfeeding: Avoid combined hormonal; use POP/IUD/condom
  • Lactational amenorrhea: <6 months + exclusive BF + amenorrhea
G3Important

Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB)

  • PALM-COEIN: Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma, Malignancy / Coagulopathy, Ovulatory, Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not classified
  • First exclude: Pregnancy (β-hCG)
  • Menorrhagia: >80 mL per cycle or >8 days
  • Metrorrhagia: irregular, between-period bleeding
  • Work-up: CBC, TSH, coagulation, prolactin, TVUS, endometrial biopsy if indicated
  • 1st line treatment: LNG-IUS; also TXA, NSAIDs, COC
  • Endometrial biopsy indications: age >45, anovulation, tamoxifen use
G4Important

Endometrial Hyperplasia

  • Caused by: unopposed estrogen (anovulation, obesity, PCOS, tamoxifen)
  • Without atypia: malignant risk <5%; treat with LNG-IUS (Mirena)
  • With atypia (EIN): malignant risk 29%; → hysterectomy if fertility not desired
  • Diagnosis: histology only — Pipelle biopsy, D&C, or hysteroscopy + biopsy
  • Follow-up: biopsy every 3–6 months until regression confirmed
  • RF: obesity, nulliparity, PCOS, late menopause, DM, Lynch syndrome
G5Routine

Menopause & HRT

  • Definition: 12 months amenorrhea from ovarian failure; avg 51 years
  • Symptoms: hot flushes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, ↓libido, osteoporosis
  • HRT indications: moderate-severe vasomotor symptoms, urogenital atrophy, surgical menopause
  • With uterus: Estrogen + progestogen (protects endometrium)
  • Without uterus: Estrogen only
  • HRT CI: breast Ca, endometrial Ca, unexplained bleeding, active VTE, liver disease
  • Lowest effective dose, shortest duration
G6Important

PCOS — Diagnosis & Management

  • Rotterdam criteria (2 of 3): irregular cycles, clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, PCO morphology on USG
  • USG: ≥20 follicles per ovary OR ovarian volume ≥10 mL
  • Labs: Total testosterone, SHBG, LH:FSH ratio >2:1
  • Exclude: pregnancy, thyroid, hyperprolactinemia, CAH (17-OHP)
  • If no fertility desired: COC (1st line) + lifestyle
  • If insulin resistance: Metformin
  • If fertility desired: Letrozole (1st line) → Clomiphene → Gonadotropins
  • Long-term risks: T2DM, endometrial Ca, CVD, depression
G7Important

Infertility — Causes & Investigation

  • Definition: No conception after 12 months (<35y) or 6 months (≥35y)
  • Female factors: ovulatory (PCOS, POI), tubal (PID, endometriosis), uterine (fibroids, polyps), cervical
  • Male factor: spermatogram — concentration, motility, morphology
  • Female work-up: TSH, prolactin, FSH/LH/E2 (day 3), AMH, AFC, TVUS, HSG
  • Tubal factor → IVF (especially bilateral hydrosalpinx)
  • Ovulatory factor → Letrozole (1st line for PCOS)
  • Uterine factor → hysteroscopy (polyps, septa, fibroids)
G8Critical

Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)

  • Minimum criteria (any 1): uterine tenderness, adnexal tenderness, cervical motion tenderness
  • Causative: N. gonorrhoeae, C. trachomatis, anaerobes
  • RF: age <25, multiple partners, prior PID, IUD
  • Treatment: Ceftriaxone IM + Doxycycline PO + Metronidazole × 14 days
  • Hospitalize if: TOA, high fever, cannot take PO, pregnancy, no improvement at 72h
  • Complications: TOA, hydrosalpinx, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain
  • Partner treatment mandatory
G9Important

Cervical Precancerous Changes (CIN)

  • Cause: Persistent high-risk HPV (16, 18, 31, 33...)
  • Screening Latvia: PAP (25–29y) → HPV test from 30y every 5y
  • CIN I: observe; colposcopy follow-up at 6/12/24 months
  • CIN II/III: cervical excision (LEEP/cone)
  • Colposcopy: acetic acid + Lugol's iodine to visualize transformation zone
  • Vaccination: 9-valent HPV vaccine from age 12 (boys and girls)
  • Invasion: refer to oncogynecologist
G10Important

Early Pregnancy Loss (Miscarriage)

  • Most common cause: chromosomal anomalies (especially T1)
  • Threatened: bleeding + closed cervix + fetal heartbeat present
  • Inevitable: bleeding + open cervix
  • Incomplete: retained products in cavity
  • Missed: CRL >7mm without heartbeat
  • Management options: expectant (7–14d), medical (misoprostol), surgical (MVA/D&C)
  • β-hCG: doubles every 48–72h in viable pregnancy; falling in miscarriage
  • Recurrent (≥3): investigate antiphospholipid syndrome, thrombophilia, uterine anomaly
G11Critical

Ectopic Pregnancy

  • Classic triad: amenorrhea + pelvic pain + vaginal bleeding
  • Most common site: ampulla of fallopian tube (95%)
  • Diagnosis: TVUS (no IUP when β-hCG ≥1500 mIU/mL) + β-hCG
  • Expectant: β-hCG <1000, stable, no cardiac activity, <35mm
  • Medical: Methotrexate 1mg/kg; CI: β-hCG >5000, cardiac activity
  • Surgical (gold standard): laparoscopic salpingectomy or salpingotomy
  • Emergency surgery: rupture, hemodynamic instability
G12Important

Uterine Fibroids (Myoma)

  • Classification: Submucosal (0–2), Intramural (3–4), Subserosal (5–7) by FIGO
  • Symptoms: PDA, dysmenorrhea, bulk symptoms (urinary, bowel), infertility
  • Asymptomatic: watchful waiting, US every 6–12 months
  • Medical: TXA, NSAIDs, LNG-IUS, GnRH agonists (pre-surgery shrinkage)
  • Submucosal → hysteroscopic myomectomy
  • Intramural/subserosal → laparoscopic myomectomy
  • Uterine artery embolization: alternative to surgery
  • Definitive: hysterectomy (no fertility plans)
G13Important

Endometriosis

  • Classic symptoms: dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, chronic pelvic pain, infertility
  • Diagnosis: TVUS (endometrioma), MRI (deep infiltrating), laparoscopy + histology (gold standard)
  • Medical: NSAIDs + COC/Dienogest/LNG-IUS for pain control
  • Severe/refractory: GnRH agonists (max 6 months, bone loss)
  • Surgical: laparoscopic excision of lesions, adhesiolysis
  • Endometrioma >3–4cm: cystectomy (not aspiration — recurs)
  • Infertility + endometriosis: consider ART; hormonal therapy does NOT improve spontaneous fertility
G14Critical

Benign Ovarian Masses & Acute Complications

  • Functional: follicular cyst (resolves in 1–3 cycles), corpus luteum cyst (rupture risk)
  • Benign neoplasms: serous/mucinous cystadenoma, dermoid (teratoma), endometrioma, fibroma
  • ALWAYS exclude first: ectopic pregnancy (β-hCG)
  • Torsion: sudden severe unilateral pain + nausea; emergency laparoscopy + detorsion
  • Ruptured cyst: sudden pain after exercise/sex; usually self-limiting
  • Malignancy features (IOTA M): irregular solid mass, ascites, ≥4 papillary structures, ≥100mm
  • Postmenopause + complex mass: CA-125 + HE4 + refer to oncogyn
